# Plate Lab Studio Footage & Stage Delivery License

discussion-draft-2026-11-18

**Discussion draft for legal review. Not an approved or executed license. Submitting an order requests a confirmed proposal; it does not authorize production use or charge a payment method.**

## 1. Parties and order schedule

The final agreement will identify the legal entity operating Plate Lab Studio (Licensor), its address, and the customer legal name and billing address (Licensee). The accepted order schedule identifies each plate serial number, source in/out frames and frame rate, named production, permitted use, target stage, deliverables, fees and agreed dates. A specifically negotiated order provision prevails over a conflicting general provision. Company identity and signing authority must be verified before execution.

## 2. Proposed license grant

Subject to an approved agreement and payment of the confirmed fees, Licensor grants a non-exclusive, worldwide, perpetual license to incorporate the listed footage into the named production and its versions, trailers and related promotion, in all media. Each project listed in the order is a separate named production. Reuse in another production, exclusivity, standalone public installations and uses outside this scope require written agreement. Copyright in the source footage remains with Licensor or its contributors.

## 3. Virtual production and VFX use

Permitted use includes displaying footage on the agreed LED walls or projection surfaces during filming, capturing that display in camera, and incorporating or compositing the footage into the named production. Editing, cropping, grading, re-projection and tiling for that production are permitted. The customer owns its independently created production, subject to the continuing ownership of the underlying footage. Stage operators may not retain the files as a general-purpose library for other productions.

## 4. Crew, vendors and secure handling

The customer may supply necessary files to its production team, stage operator, editor and VFX or post-production vendors solely to perform work on the named production. The customer must ensure those recipients comply with the agreed use and protect the files from unauthorized access. Distributing the completed production is permitted; transferring reusable footage assets to unrelated parties is not. Source files may not be resold, sublicensed as stock, posted for download or added to shared asset libraries.

## 5. Previews, clearance and sensitive content

Watermarked previews and Lab 360 renders are for evaluation and planning, not final production use. Models, lighting and stage simulations are illustrative and are not supplied assets unless listed in the order. Brands, signage, people, buildings, artwork and incidental audio may require additional permissions. Only releases or clearances expressly identified in the final order are represented as supplied. The customer determines whether its intended context requires additional clearance and must not imply endorsement by depicted parties.

## 6. Target stage and deliverables

Custom delivery is built for the stage and screen specification confirmed in the order. The customer supplies the facility and stage name or measured wall geometry, units, height, diameter or width, wrap angle where relevant, upper/ceiling display type, and any pixel map or processor requirements. Plate Lab confirms output resolution, frame rate, codec, color space and HDR/SDR handling before rendering. Camera originals, RAW files, separate camera feeds, project files and additional stage versions are excluded unless expressly itemized. Changes to confirmed specifications may require a revised price and schedule.

## 7. Order confirmation, fees and cancellation

Checkout is a request for review, not automatic acceptance, reservation, payment or license issuance. The preliminary invoice records the current estimate; taxes, customization scope and any additional services remain subject to confirmation. Work starts only after written scope approval, final licensing arrangements and agreed payment requirements. The final order must state the payment timetable and cancellation terms. No cancellation fee or non-refundable deposit is created by this draft.

## 8. Delivery, verification and changes

Delivery uses the secure method agreed in the order, with a file manifest and technical specifications. The customer should check files promptly against the agreed specification and report reproducible defects. Plate Lab will assess and correct verified departures from that specification under the remedy agreed in the final contract. Artistic changes, additional grades, changed stage geometry and new source ranges are separately scoped. The final contract must define the inspection period, revision allowance, defect remedy and any refund right; this draft does not set a binding deadline.

## 9. Retention and backups

The proposed operational policy is to keep rendered delivery files available for 30 days after delivery, with that expiry stated in the delivery notice. The customer should download and maintain its own backups. A later re-render or redelivery may require a new quote. This proposed retention period must be approved in the final agreement and must not limit any mandatory rights or separately agreed archive obligation.

## 10. Rights and assurances for counsel to finalize

The intended assurance is that Licensor has authority to grant the expressly stated footage rights, and that the delivered files will substantially match the confirmed technical specification. Release coverage, territorial limits and disclosed restrictions must be recorded per order. No promise is made in this draft that every depicted element is cleared, that metadata is error-free, or that a preview precisely predicts the final stage image. Counsel must settle the warranty language, exclusions and remedies before the agreement is used.

## 11. Claims, liability and insurance - unresolved

The final contract must allocate responsibility for claims arising from footage ownership, customer modifications, unapproved uses and third-party clearances. Counsel should determine appropriate defense and indemnity procedures, financial caps, insurance requirements, exclusions and exceptions, including fraud, deliberate misconduct and liabilities that cannot legally be excluded. No indemnity obligation or liability cap is agreed by this draft.

## 12. AI and prohibited uses

The proposed license does not authorize using the footage or metadata to train or fine-tune a machine-learning model, build a stock-media substitute, or distribute a reusable dataset without written permission. Ordinary production tools used to edit or finish the named production are not prohibited solely because they contain AI features, provided the provider does not acquire independent training or redistribution rights in the supplied files. Unlawful uses and removal of rights-management information to misrepresent ownership are prohibited.

## 13. Termination and completed productions

The final agreement should provide written notice and an appropriate opportunity to remedy material breach, with urgent suspension limited to circumstances counsel identifies. It should specify treatment of completed and already distributed productions, contractor copies, backups and outstanding payment obligations. The customer must not be required by this draft to recall an already distributed production.

## 14. Final agreement and execution

Licensor legal identity, governing law, forum, notice addresses, signatures, privacy responsibilities, tax treatment and any studio purchasing terms remain to be completed by the parties and counsel. This document is a negotiating starting point. A checkout acknowledgment records that the customer understands the order-review process; it is not acceptance of this draft as a binding license. The approved license version and final order schedule must be expressly accepted before production use is authorized.
